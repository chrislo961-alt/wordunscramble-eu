How to turn your clues into filters

The solver is most useful when every clue has one clear job. Put confirmed-position letters into the five-character pattern. Add letters that are definitely present but whose position is unknown under Included letters. Put letters that cannot occur anywhere in the answer under Excluded letters.

For a yellow clue, you can also record the position that letter cannot occupy. For example, a1 means the answer contains A but A is not in position 1. Multiple rules can be separated by spaces, such as a1 i4.

Lock green letters

Use a pattern such as ?r??e to keep R in position 2 and E in position 5.

Place yellow letters

Add the letter under Included, then optionally note a forbidden position such as a1.

Remove grey letters

Enter every confirmed absent letter so the candidate list shrinks quickly.

Common English or broad English?

Start with Common English when you want plausible everyday answers first. Switch to Broad English if the common list produces too few options or you suspect a rarer word. The broad list is intentionally larger and may include specialist or uncommon entries.

Why the order of clues matters

A strong pattern usually removes more candidates than a long excluded-letter list. If you know exact positions, enter those first. Then add included letters, then grey letters. If too many results remain, record yellow-position restrictions rather than adding guesses that are not fully confirmed.

Example

Suppose the pattern is ?R??E, you know A is somewhere in the answer but not first, and S, T, O and L are absent. Enter ?r??e, put a under Included, stol under Excluded, and a1 under Wrong positions. That describes the clue state directly instead of approximating it.

FAQ

Does this know the official daily answer?

No. It filters the site's English word data. Individual daily games may use a smaller private answer list, so the tool is designed to narrow possibilities rather than reveal a guaranteed official answer.

Can the same letter appear twice?

Yes. The solver does not block repeated letters. Be careful with grey clues when a letter appears more than once in a guess, because a grey tile can sometimes mean there are no additional copies rather than that the letter is completely absent.

What should I do if I still have hundreds of matches?

Add another confirmed clue, especially a known position or a yellow-position restriction. You can also use the Wordle pattern strategy guide for a more systematic way to reduce candidates.
