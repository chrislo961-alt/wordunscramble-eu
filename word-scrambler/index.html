How to make a useful word scramble

Enter a word, generate a shuffled version, and copy the result into a worksheet, message or puzzle. For a fair challenge, keep every original letter exactly once and avoid giving away the answer with punctuation or spacing.

Good uses for scrambled words

Vocabulary practice

Give learners a scrambled spelling after introducing a new word, then ask them to rebuild it without a word bank.

Warm-up puzzles

Use short familiar words for quick classroom, meeting or party icebreakers.

Theme rounds

Create several scrambles from the same topic—animals, countries, tools or foods—so solvers have an extra contextual clue.

Choosing the right difficulty

Short words with repeated letters are often easier than long words with many distinct letters. If the scramble looks too close to the original spelling, generate another arrangement. For younger solvers, add a category clue or reveal the first letter.

Scrambler vs. unscrambler

This page creates the puzzle. If you already have mixed-up letters and want possible solutions, use the Word Unscrambler. If you want exact same-letter rearrangements and filtering, use the Anagram Solver.

Word Scrambler FAQ

Does the scrambler change the letters?

No. It rearranges the characters you enter rather than adding or removing letters.

Can I use it for phrases?

You can enter longer text, but the tool works best with single words or compact phrases where the letter jumble is still readable.

Why do I sometimes get a similar-looking scramble?

Random shuffling can occasionally place several letters near their original positions. Press Scramble again to generate a different arrangement.
