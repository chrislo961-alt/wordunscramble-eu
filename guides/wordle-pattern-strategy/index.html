A better Wordle pattern strategy

The fastest way to narrow a five-letter puzzle is to keep three types of information separate: letters fixed in position, letters known to exist somewhere else, and letters ruled out entirely.

Green letters: lock the pattern first

Write green letters in their exact positions and use a wildcard for everything unknown. A clue such as C _ A _ E becomes c?a?e. This immediately removes every word with the wrong length or fixed letters in the wrong slots.

Yellow letters: include them without fixing them

A yellow letter belongs in the word but not in the position where you tried it. The solver’s included-letter field is useful for this because it requires the letter somewhere without assigning a slot. Mentally, however, remember the forbidden position too; the current simple solver does not model every yellow-position exclusion separately.

Grey letters: exclude carefully

Grey letters can eliminate a large part of the dictionary, but repeated letters require caution. If you guessed a word containing two copies of a letter and only one was marked as present, a grey copy does not necessarily mean the letter is absent from the answer. Use the game’s feedback rules before excluding a repeated letter completely.

Choose guesses that reveal information

When many candidates remain, a guess that tests several new letters may be more useful than immediately guessing one possible answer. Common consonants and vowels can split a long candidate list into much smaller groups. Once only a few candidates remain, switch from information-gathering to answer selection.

Example workflow

Suppose you know the pattern ? R ? A ?, the word contains T, and it does not contain B, D, F or M. Enter the fixed pattern first, then T as included, then the excluded letters. If the remaining list is still long, compare which letters differ between the candidates and choose a guess that tests several of them at once.
