Broad, Common or ENABLE?

A word finder is only as useful as the list behind it. WordUnscramble.eu offers different modes because “is this an English word?” and “is this likely to be the answer?” are not the same question.

Common English: best first choice for most people

Common mode favors familiar words and removes much of the specialist, archaic and unusual vocabulary found in larger dictionaries. It is usually the best starting point for classroom puzzles, casual anagrams and everyday word searches because it keeps the answer list readable.

Broad English: maximum coverage

Broad mode searches the larger open English index used by the site. It is useful when a puzzle expects unusual vocabulary, when you are researching possible forms, or when Common mode finds nothing. The tradeoff is noise: a broad list can include rare, regional, historical or specialist entries that are technically present in the source data but unlikely to be an intended everyday answer.

ENABLE: an open game-oriented list

ENABLE is the Enhanced North American Benchmark Lexicon, a public-domain word list historically used by open word-game projects. It is useful as a game-oriented comparison set, but it is not presented here as the current official dictionary of Scrabble, Words With Friends or any commercial tournament system.

Which should you choose?

Everyday puzzle

Start with Common English. Broaden only if the expected answer is missing.

Research or obscure clue

Use Broad English and then verify any unfamiliar result in a dictionary.

Word-game exploration

ENABLE can be a useful open reference, but check the specific game’s rules for official validity.

Why results can disagree with another website

Different dictionaries make different editorial choices. They may disagree about inflected forms, regional spellings, proper names, abbreviations, archaic words and specialist terminology. A word appearing here means it is present in the selected site list; it does not mean every dictionary or game accepts it.

Our recommendation

Use Common mode when you want a useful answer quickly. Switch to Broad only when you need coverage, and treat unfamiliar broad-list results as candidates to verify rather than automatic answers.
